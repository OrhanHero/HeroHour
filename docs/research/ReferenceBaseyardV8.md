# Reference Baseyard V8

This pass dresses the existing start-base ground around the reference HQs. It follows the supplied `01_22_20.png` base concept's used concrete, service markings, drainage and dusty edges. Actual V7 base and HQ close-up screenshots were inspected before modelling. No commercial game assets are used.

## Layout and scope

`BattlefieldLayout.h` supplies HQ positions (-8000,0) and (8000,0). `PrototypeEconomy::Restart` supplies depots (-7500,1600)/(7500,1600) and power plants (-9000,-1600)/(9000,-1600). The existing saved-map audit confirms 18m HQ aprons and 9m depot pads. Their asphalt contact surface is at world Z5cm. The build script reads the cached audit in `Art/Models/RoadFinishV6/scene-input.json` and records its SHA256 and relevant actors in the V8 manifest.

Two independently exported merged meshes are placed at the HQ origins with identity rotation and scale. The eastern layout mirrors X to match its opposite depot/power offsets; its moved Tempest field is excluded separately. The source also compensates the established Blender-FBX Y reflection. The result does not depend on negative runtime scaling or guessed positional Rotator arguments.

The geometry adds fitted, lightly irregular concrete slabs and seams around the existing HQ foundation; narrow depot service slabs; shallow drain channels with actual grates; restrained maintenance paint; worn twin tire marks; soft-edged dirt deposition; and tiny stone chips. Building foundations remain clear. Existing main streets outside the pads, the western curved approach and both starting resource fields are protected. The power-plant site's small work-surface ring surrounds the existing footprint; it does not add a building or a gameplay reservation.

All additions are decorative and at most 3cm above their contact surface. Exterior stone chips are at most 2.8cm above ground. The higher original apron curbs/bollards visible in previews already belong to the existing map. They are not part of V8. Asphalt-pad details occupy their actual Z5cm contact level rather than being buried under it.

## Materials and cost

Each merged mesh has six material sections. Six local material instances share a normal surface parent and a specialized opaque dust parent under `/Game/HERO/ReferenceV8/Baseyard/Materials`. They reuse the project's own DistrictV3 Albedo/Normal/Roughness textures with physical metre UV0 mapping. Concrete repairs have two restrained tonal variants; steel, deposited dirt, rubber wear and muted paint use distinct PBR settings. No global material is edited.

Dust uses a checked vertex-alpha falloff to blend its dirt PBR surface into world-space sand at the perimeter. This is a fully opaque material blend: no transparency, opacity mask, pixel offset or emissive lighting. The original hard polygonal dirt islands were rejected during preview review. The falloff attribute is checked after a real FBX roundtrip and imported with vertex colors set to Replace. Actual Unreal ground blending still needs review because the map's underlying ground contains additional gravel/dirt layers.

Small geometry islands are merged into two StaticMeshActors, not spawned as individual stone/grate actors. Both actors must use NoCollision, `can_ever_affect_navigation=False`, `cast_shadow=False` and no distance-field lighting contribution. Imports keep `auto_generate_collision=False`. The importer verifies every reflected `UBodySetup.AggGeom` shape array is empty and snapshots every other map actor's transform, mesh/material bindings and collision state to verify they remain unchanged.

`StaticMeshEditorSubsystem` is unavailable in this project's NullRHI Python commandlet. The deprecated `EditorStaticMeshLibrary` delegates to that same subsystem, so it does not solve this case. The importer instead reads the reflected BodySetup/AggGeom properties directly, covering all nine shape arrays listed in the installed UE5.8 `PhysicsEngine/AggregateGeom.h` (including convex and experimental level-set types). A missing BodySetup means zero shapes, matching the native editor collision-count implementation. Unreadable fields or nonempty aggregates fail before map changes are saved. An interactive editor may remove existing shapes through its available subsystem, followed by the same full aggregate check; the commandlet does not clear reflected arrays without native physics-cache invalidation. Per-mesh shape counts and the validation path are written to the import report.

The final source export contains 13,764 triangles in the west mesh and 11,662 in the east mesh, for 25,426 triangles across both bases. Maximum added relief is 2.8cm. The 1,295 disconnected detail islands are contained inside those two meshes with six shared material instances, rather than creating that many scene actors. Both FBX roundtrips preserve bounds and the dust falloff channel, with zero non-manifold edges or degenerate source faces. Build completion was observed in `Saved/Logs/ReferenceBaseyardV8-Blender.log`; Unreal acceptance is separate.

## Reproduce and integrate

1. Run Blender 5.2 with `Scripts/art/build_reference_baseyard_v8.py`. Require the `REFERENCE_BASEYARD_V8_BUILD_PASS` marker and no traceback. Inspect both `*-Preview.png` and `*-HQCloseUp.png` images under `Art/Models/ReferenceBaseyardV8/`.
2. Read the adjacent mesh JSONs and `manifest.json`. They contain triangle counts, source/mirrored Unreal bounds, piece categories, exact actor locations, FBX hashes, relief limits, UV and manifold checks.
3. Root executes `Scripts/art/import_reference_baseyard_v8.py` through Unreal Python, serially with other map mutations. This importer is prepared but was not executed by the art agent. It preflights source hashes, texture inputs and both existing HQ aprons, imports only its own two meshes/materials, and replaces only actors with the `BYV8 ` prefix or `HERO_BaseyardV8` tag.
4. Require `REFERENCE_BASEYARD_V8_IMPORT_PASS` and inspect `Saved/Research/ReferenceBaseyardV8-Import.json`. Confirm two actors, six shared material instances, no collision/nav/shadows and unchanged existing actor snapshots.
5. Capture current Unreal base/collector views, inspect front contact, tire scale and dust blending, then run the package's navigation, economy and presentation checks. Blender imagery does not establish runtime acceptance.

The build saves each source blend before adding preview context. Previews contain read-only copies of existing HQ, refinery, power plant and pad meshes to show real proportions and foundation clearance. Their surfaces use source palettes rather than final runtime materials; they are clearly Blender renders, not game screenshots. Exterior tire wear uses lighter displaced dust, while rubber marks on the existing apron remain darker. The exported V8 files contain only the new flat ground details.

## Remaining limits

The existing broad square pad silhouettes are retained. Slab joints, mechanical drains and small dirt transitions improve their surface treatment; they do not reproduce the complete rocky terrain, dense base props or destruction in the concepts. Tire wear is static and follows plausible starting travel space rather than recording live vehicle movement. No tire deformation, particles or animated puddles are introduced. Performance and final ground-color matching must be measured in Unreal after Root integrates the prepared assets.

## Actual Unreal review, 16 October 2026

Root's retry import passed. `docs/verification/2026-10-16/reference-v8/baseyard/Import.json` preserves the actual report: two actors, six material instances, 25,426 triangles, all nine collision aggregate arrays empty, navigation unchanged, and 1,298 existing actors preserved. `VisualEvidence.json` identifies the two reviewed screenshots, original concept and import log by SHA256. These are Editor runtime captures; they do not establish packaged-build tests or performance.

The reviewed images are `Art/Previews/ReferenceV8-Baseyard-Aegis-1600.png` and `ReferenceV8-Baseyard-Ember-HQCloseUp.png`. The reference is the supplied `Codex-Bild 1. Sept. 2026, 01_22_20.png`, viewed directly from the original E: folder. The Aegis image shows fitted panel divisions surrounding the HQ, a dark drainage strip on the near/right edge, restrained yellow service markings, and a more believable connection between the building's foundation and courtyard. The Ember close view confirms those same details remain visible around its larger dark silhouette. The new ground details are grounded and do not visibly cut through either HQ. Fine tire wear and dirt feathering are too subtle at these views to certify their quality separately.

The concept remains substantially richer. Its ground contains broken asphalt, varied rocks, dust, debris and irregular terrain transitions; the current image is dominated by broad, smooth yellow sand and repeated clean roads. Existing square pads still read as isolated display plinths. Background city buildings repeat almost identical shallow roof blocks, while the concept has varied architectural masses. The dark HQ retains large simplified roof planes and straight service wings; its concept has more angled buttresses, cylindrical machinery and layered panels. No moving wheels, animated harvesting or physical base activity can be inferred from a still image. This pass is a visible surface improvement, not concept parity.

## Highest-impact next original environment asset

Build a reusable **rocky base-edge and roadside kit** before adding further sub-centimetre courtyard detail. It addresses the largest visible mismatch: empty, uniformly flat sand surrounding otherwise increasingly detailed buildings. Proposed source ownership is one art agent owning `Scripts/art/build_reference_terrain_edges_v9.py` and `Art/Models/ReferenceTerrainEdgesV9/`, with a separate importer `Scripts/art/import_reference_terrain_edges_v9.py` executed only by Root. This proposal creates no files or map changes yet.

The kit should contain three 4m-wide low embankment sections (straight, convex and concave; 1.5m depth, 0.35–0.8m height), two rubble groups (1.2m and 2.4m wide; 0.15–0.4m height), and three individual eroded boulders (0.4m, 0.8m and 1.4m). Use original sculpted angular strata, varied silhouettes and UV-baked rock/sand material detail. Target 2–5k triangles per embankment and 300–1,500 per prop, shared PBR textures, grounded pivots and explicit bounds. Place only outside road lanes, buildable areas and resource routes verified against actual map reservations; the kit must never conceal a collision/navigation discrepancy. Preview in the real base camera before acceptance. The intended result is the concept's rocky, inhabited desert setting while preserving the current gameplay clearances.
